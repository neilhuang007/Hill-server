# Hill housing facade and material research — 5 September 2026

This pass accounts for 25 individually identified housing footprints, with measured plans and source-indexed roof review data. It provides close or partial photo evidence where available, and explicit unknowns elsewhere. It does **not** establish that all elevations are photographed, all material products are identified, or all buildings are visually complete in Minecraft. The material-first user instruction superseded expansion of aperture proposals during this pass.

The largest immediate corrections are the western houses: Thomas is visibly brick; Feroe has cream rendered-looking surfaces and a stone lower wall; the Business Office has rough gray stucco-looking surfaces. Giving all three a brick exterior would contradict the actual captured views. These are August 2019 Google photographs, not evidence of a 2026 repaint or a manufacturer specification. ## Metric construction proposals and roof corrections

The shared physical frame is X east, Z south, metres, at origin latitude 40.24516 / longitude −75.63516; heights are NAVD88. Local coordinates follow `XZ = origin + u·U + v·V`. The construction proposal side names use local axes: north = −V, south = +V, west = −U, east = +U. They are approximate compass labels on rotated buildings. Export at the approved 2 blocks/m uses Y = 2 × (NAVD88 − 25), with quantization handled by the builder.

Normalized `construction_proposal` objects are stable for **Foster ix 31, Rolfe ix 25, East Faculty unit 3 ix 14, East Faculty duplex 4/5 ix 15, and Sherrill ix 67**. They include entrance/floor NAVD88, window rows with side/plane/centre/sill/width/height/lights/shape, doors, porch/dormer bounds where justified, and source flags on every proposed row. Numeric aperture positions are interpretations scaled to measured plans, not survey measurements. The other twenty parents retain descriptive/metric evidence but do not yet have a complete normalized aperture proposal. This gap is explicit and must not be reported as facade completion.

Foster and Rolfe’s lower reconstructed base elevations are not their sports-field entrances. Thirty DEM samples in each north approach, distributed across five positions and 1–18 m out from the facade, cluster around 67.90–68.24 m NAVD88. The proposed front entry is 68.1 m, while the rear ground falls markedly, especially behind Rolfe. The per-parent profile data are available for joining the real terrain without lifting or shifting the footprint. Lower rear window/door levels remain proposed and need a native visual audit.

The raw roof maximum is not a safe building-height control for the western houses. The table lists broad roof planes and separately flagged high fragments, with 0-based face IDs referring to the exact ordered source list in `measured.roof_planes`. They are review sets rather than a destructive cut. Source CityJSON SHA-256 is `b5dd39b48b012224388a9d1310b22897ebca9ca40c1bcb5a4ef77075602478e8`.

## Colour measurements and construction materials

The appearance ledger samples native photo pixels without exposure or white-balance correction. Every sample carries its source image hash, exact rectangle, size, semantic component and confidence note. sRGB is linearized using the standard transfer function, converted to XYZ D65 and then CIELAB. Median colour and P10/P90 quantify photographed variation; they do not measure intrinsic paint reflectance. Lit and shaded wall samples remain separate.

Examples illustrate why semantic material identity must precede colour matching: Thomas’s shaded brick sample has median RGB (88, 67, 56), Feroe’s lit smooth cream wall (179, 172, 146), and the Business Office’s unobstructed rough wall (153, 149, 146). White paint in shaded Street View can measure blue-gray. Meigs’s lit and deep-porch wall samples also differ strongly. Matching a shaded wall directly to the darkest generic block would discard the actual facade material and lighting context. These values belong to these photographs only. [Thomas](https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&pano=YOcyA3Ibp7ZeEVWbmmm68Q&heading=90&pitch=0&fov=70), [Feroe](https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&pano=cODrD_pgaOcm6hJCtBNbhQ&heading=90&pitch=0&fov=70), [Business Office](https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&pano=MaglwSohOgIjzpPSF6LNTA&heading=90&pitch=0&fov=70), [Meigs](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1706640886/thehillorg/jw0ysayvvi02ln5dsucr/FY24HTFlipCampaignInsertPRINTedits.pdf).

No Minecraft blocks or palettes are selected here. The material ledger distinguishes an actual named material supported by text (for example Foster/Rolfe brick) from an observed surface profile (horizontal siding, stucco-looking render, stone-pattern facing) and unresolved substrate. A gray overlapping roof is not automatically slate; siding cannot be declared fiber cement from colour; and a masonry appearance is not a geological stone identification.

Additional clean samples cover Hunt’s upper brick, lower brown masonry, both red roof slopes and green cornice, plus Alumni House brick, decorative pale fishscale-profile gable cladding and cream porch posts. Hunt’s visible red overlapping units are tile-like, but the exact substrate is not proved by these photographs. The architect plan identifies brownstone masonry on Hunt; the Alumni architect photo identifies appearance but no cladding-product specification. [Hunt contractor photo](https://wohlsenconstruction.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/k-12-schools-construction-the-hill-school-upper-school-dormitory-renovation-1.jpg), [Architect plan](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/hilll-school-master-plan-2013/58408750), [Alumni architect photo](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5988a923c534a5f2895959c1/1541532697942-UB6KC7K5VBA4ZJ3WVLMT/Hill%2BAlumni%2BExt%2BAlt%2B2.jpg).
